How it works

Twenty-one points.
Everything else is math.

Google’s MediaPipe finds 21 points on your hand in every frame. Our own code decides what they mean.

CAMERA

Front camera

CameraX streams frames. Only the newest frame is kept so nothing lags.

MEDIAPIPE

Hand landmarks

An on-device model returns 21 points and its own guess at the gesture.

ENGINE

Gesture engine

Finger geometry, hold-to-confirm and swipe detection turn points into poses.

ACTION

Phone control

Android services press, swipe, type and adjust the system.

The story

A college project
that kept growing.

Air Gestures began as a question: can a phone camera replace the touch screen for small tasks? It started as one screen that recognised a few poses, and grew one upgrade at a time.

START

A camera and a few poses

The first version recognised hand poses with MediaPipe and could open an app or change the volume.

VOICE + BACKGROUND

Speak to it, use it anywhere

Voice commands and a floating overlay let it work above other apps.

STABILITY

Fewer false triggers

Hold-to-confirm, hysteresis for pinches and new gestures made it feel dependable.

PERSONAL

Remap, play, measure

Every gesture became assignable. A challenge game and a stats page arrived.

REELS

The L sign

An accessibility service let a hand shape scroll Instagram Reels.

POLISH

Animations, tour, sound, battery saver

A new splash screen, an onboarding tour, a glass overlay and smoother motion.

ACTIONS

Double tap and system actions

Three fingers like a reel. Screenshot, back, recents and notifications became assignable.

AUTOPILOT

Voice that works inside apps

Multi-step commands open apps, search, click and type, and wait for your “send”.

OPEN SOURCE

Hand-only stage and GitHub

The camera preview gave way to an animated stage, and the code went public on GitHub.

Privacy

Your hand stays
on your phone.

  • ✓On-device AI. Hand tracking runs on the phone. No images or video leave it.
  • ✓Screen reading only on request. The accessibility service looks at button labels only when you give a voice command, and never stores or sends them.
  • ✓Nothing sends by itself. Messages are typed, then wait for you to say “send”.
  • ✓Read every line. The whole project is open source under the MIT licence.

Install

Try it in two minutes.

  1. Download the APK from the Releases page on GitHub and open it on your Android phone.
  2. Allow “Install unknown apps” when asked. Android needs this for apps outside the Play Store.
  3. Allow camera and microphone when the app asks.
  4. Optional: for reel scrolling, double tap and Voice Autopilot, enable the accessibility service. On Android 13 or newer, first open App info, tap ⋮, then Allow restricted settings.
Play Protect says “App blocked”

Google Play Protect can block apps outside the Play Store that use accessibility. Open the Play Store, tap your profile picture, then Play Protect, then the settings icon, and turn off “Scan apps with Play Protect” for the install. Turn it back on afterwards. Or build the app yourself, which never shows the warning.

Which Android versions work?

Android 8.0 or newer. Screenshots need Android 9, and pressing the search key in Voice Autopilot needs Android 11.

Build it from source

Clone the repository, open it in Android Studio, let Gradle sync, plug in your phone and press Run. Full steps are in the README.
